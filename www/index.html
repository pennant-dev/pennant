A day with Pennant

By the time you're up, the work is done.

Maya runs Harbor, a small software company. Here's an ordinary Tuesday with Pennant, played the way the app does it. The people, the company and the numbers are made up.

A fix, tested and opened as a pull request.

"Imports are slow again, can you look?" Code changes go to a coding run in a thread of its own, with Claude Code or Pennant's own engine, in its own copy of the repository. It finds the slow path, fixes it, runs the tests and opens the pull request as its own GitHub App, never as Maya. Deleting a branch still waits for her.

      Trust

      Built so you can leave it running.

      Agents fail in familiar ways: they send before they should, retry what already went out, and drift from what they were asked. Pennant is built around those.

      It asks first.

      Publishing, sending, deleting and spending stop at a card, whatever a skill says or the model decides. Approving runs exactly what the card shows.

      Instructions can't widen what it may do.

      Permissions come from the harness, from sign-off rules and grants, not from what a prompt or a web page tells the model.

      One owner of the screen.

      When it needs your real pointer it takes a lease on the screen. Touch the mouse and it lets go.

      Uncertain means check, not retry.

      If a crash leaves it unsure whether something went out, it looks before it tries again.

      A budget and a ledger.

      Every task and goal has an allowance of steps, tokens and time, and every run is costed by job and by model.

      Secrets stay out of the chat.

      Keys and passwords live in your Keychain. The model never sees them.

      Yours

      Free, open source, and on your Mac.

      Pennant is about 69,000 lines of Swift under the Apache 2.0 licence: a host that runs the agent, the Mac and iPhone apps, and a command line.

      • No account with us, no telemetry, no server of ours in the middle.
      • Everything in one folder you own. Export and import move it to your next Mac.
      • Your model, your choice. With a local model, nothing leaves your Mac.
      • The iPhone talks to your Mac directly, over your network or Tailscale.
