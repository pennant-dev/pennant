The AI agent that does the work.

Pennant is one open-source agent that lives on your Mac. Type to it or just talk. It runs your jobs on a schedule, remembers what you told it, uses your apps the way you do, writes and ships code, splits big jobs across workers and connects to the services you use, with any model you choose. Anything it would publish, send, delete or pay for waits for your OK.

Talk mode

Talk it through, out loud.

Press the waveform in the Pennant chat and just talk. Pennant answers in a few spoken sentences, in a natural voice that runs on your Mac's own GPU. Nothing you say goes to a cloud voice.

  • Waits for a real pause, so a breath mid-sentence doesn't send half a thought.
  • Long answers, said short: a report becomes what happened and what you need to do.
  • Talk over it to stop it, and mute yourself while it keeps going.
  • Pick a voice: Penny, Kokoro's American and British voices, or Qwen's expressive speakers.
  • On your iPhone too. Your Mac makes the voice and streams it to the phone.

Jobs and threads

Give it jobs. Every run gets a thread of its own.

Tell Pennant what to do and when, in plain words or cron. Each run happens in its own thread, so two jobs never land in the same conversation. A run with nothing to say closes itself; anything that needs you goes to the top of the list. Press a job's Run.

Schedules 5 jobsNew job
Threads

You talk to Pennant, in one chat. It answers what it can and starts a thread for the rest, then tells you what came of it, in its own words. Each job follows a skill, a folder of instructions and scripts you can read and change. Big jobs bring in workers on a cheaper model, and code changes go to a coding run in your folder.

Built for failure

Built for the ways agents actually fail.

Demos stop at the happy path. Pennant was built around the unhappy ones, because every one of these happened to us first.

Sends first, apologizes later

It asks before it acts.

Publishing, sending email, deleting and spending always stop at a card, whatever the skill or the model says. Approving runs exactly what the card shows. You can also edit the text, send it back with a note, or reject it.

Stuck for hours, and nobody knows

A heartbeat.

Every 30 minutes, or what you choose, Pennant looks over its work on its own: it starts the goal sessions that are due, and anything stuck or waiting on you too long gets dealt with or brought to you. A look with nothing in it costs nothing.

Looked over the work2 threads nudged
One 429 and the run is gone

Rate limits don't end the run.

On a 429, Pennant backs off, then pauses the task and resumes it on its own, even after a restart. When a provider goes down, that model rests for three minutes and the task moves to your next one.

Rate limitedresumes in 1:52
The surprise bill

Every run has a price.

Tokens and dollars for every run, job and model, with cache hits counted and big runs flagged. Prompts are built so providers can cache them: 82% of input tokens on our daily runs.

Secrets stay out of the chat.

The vault hands credentials to a skill's scripts as environment variables, never to the model, and scrubs them from everything the scripts print.

Two jobs, one agent, chaos

Schedules that don't collide.

Every run gets its own thread, so a job that fires while Pennant is busy never lands in the middle of another. A job whose last run is still going skips its turn and says why.

Takes over your screen

It works beside you.

On the web, Pennant works in tabs of its own in your Chrome, with your sign-ins; your pointer, keyboard and tabs stay yours. It works in your Mac apps in the background, behind your windows. When it truly needs the screen, touch the mouse and it stops.

It never guesses twice.

If Pennant stops in the middle of an action, the action is marked uncertain, and the same call is refused until the agent has checked what actually happened.

Every fact keeps where it came from and when. Something Pennant inferred never overwrites something you said, and a fact you forget stays forgotten.

Models and cost

Any model. Your Mac. Every cent accounted for.

Point Pennant at an API, a GPU box on your desk, a local model through Ollama, or Apple's on-device model. Give it a primary model, fallbacks and a cheaper one for workers, and it routes around rate limits, outages and spent quotas. Everything it knows stays in one folder on your Mac and moves to your next one with a single export.

iPhone
